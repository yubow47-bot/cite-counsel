# Citation scope and source claims
> Choose citation scope from the request and source material without inventing missing locators.

A citation to a whole work needs no pinpoint. A pinpoint identifies a smaller
part and should come from the user's request or from a location established in
that same work. A paragraph number in a different document does not establish
the requested work's paragraph number.

Keep these claims separate: a formatter can produce the required syntax; a
record can identify where fields came from; a quotation checker can compare
words with one retrieved text. None of those alone proves that the source is
current, authoritative, complete, or applicable to the user's legal question.

For example, if the user requests a citation to an Act as a whole, omit an
unrequested section number. If a source lacks a needed field, report the gap
instead of supplying a plausible value.
