Canadian Environmental Assessment Agency — Policy Framework

Regional Assessments under the Impact Assessment Act

Overview

Regional assessments are studies conducted in areas of existing or anticipated project development to inform the planning and management of cumulative effects. Regional assessments are conducted by a committee or by the Agency alone, following an agreement between the Minister of Environment and Climate Change and the relevant provincial, territorial, or Indigenous governing body.

Purpose

The purpose of a regional assessment is to provide a comprehensive understanding of the environmental, health, social, and economic conditions in a specific region, including the potential cumulative effects of existing and future physical activities. Regional assessments contribute to more efficient and effective project assessments by providing baseline information, identifying valued components, and establishing thresholds and targets for cumulative effects management.

How Regional Assessments Are Conducted

The Minister may enter into an agreement with any jurisdiction to establish a committee for the purpose of conducting a regional assessment. The terms of reference for a regional assessment are established through consultation with federal authorities, provincial and territorial governments, Indigenous peoples, and the public. Once completed, the regional assessment report is made publicly available and can inform subsequent project assessments under the Impact Assessment Act.

Current Regional Assessments

The Agency is currently conducting regional assessments in several regions across Canada, including the Ring of Fire region in northern Ontario and offshore wind development areas off the Atlantic coast.

For more information on ongoing regional assessments, including terms of reference, interim reports, and public comment opportunities, visit the Canadian Impact Assessment Registry.
