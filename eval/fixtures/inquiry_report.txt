Commission of Inquiry into the Regulation of Digital Platforms

Final Report

Submitted to the Governor in Council pursuant to Part I of the Inquiries Act
December 2025

The Commission of Inquiry into the Regulation of Digital Platforms was established by Order in Council P.C. 2024-0312 on March 15, 2024, with the Honourable Justice Marianne Dubois appointed as Commissioner.

The Commission was mandated to inquire into and report on: the adequacy of existing federal regulatory frameworks for digital platforms operating in Canada; the impact of algorithmic content distribution on public discourse and democratic institutions; the competitive dynamics of digital advertising markets; and the effectiveness of current mechanisms for addressing harmful online content.

Over the course of eighteen months, the Commission held public hearings in twelve cities across Canada, received over three hundred written submissions from stakeholders, and commissioned independent research from experts in computer science, economics, media studies, and constitutional law.

The Commission's report contains forty-two recommendations, including the establishment of a new Digital Platform Regulator with the authority to enforce transparency obligations, impose penalties for non-compliance, and conduct systemic investigations into platform practices that may undermine public trust in digital communication and commerce.

The recommendations are organized into five chapters addressing regulatory architecture, content governance, competition and market structure, privacy and data protection, and democratic integrity.
